# Living Horizon

**Living Horizon** is a Minecraft mod that keeps mobs and players visible far past your
render distance, through several rendering techniques. Above all, it draws them on the
terrain generated by **Voxy** and **Distant Horizons**, so the far land is no longer empty.
Depending on your settings, it can also help performance: play with a lower simulation
distance and still see a living world.

## Features

### Mobs on the horizon

Every mob can now be seen from far away, on the game's own terrain and **on the terrain
generated by Voxy and Distant Horizons**. Any entity drawn the way Minecraft draws its own
can be shown in the distance, mobs from other mods included (pick them in *Mobs &
Entities...*). They graze, wander and look around, each in its own way.

### Players at any distance

Other players stay visible at any distance, as they are up close: with their gear, their
mount, and so on.

**Players who logged off live on.** When a player logs off, a copy of them appears with
their name and goes on with their life where they left: strolling, sitting, opening a chest,
sleeping in a bed at night, raising a shield against monsters. The world feels even more
alive, until they come back.

### Birds in the sky

Birds fly over the land and can be seen from far away: flocks, geese in a V, birds of prey,
gulls over the coast, ducks on the water, bats at night... They land on trees, buildings,
fields and beaches, and take off again when you come near.

## Performance

A mod that adds this many figures has to stay light, so it never simulates them:

- **Mobs**: their AI is off, for good. The mod reads the ground around each mob once, then
  plays predefined animations that fit there: a stroll, a pause to graze, a look around.
- **Players who logged off**: up close, a small AI lets them use what is around them (beds,
  chests, workstations). Farther away, they play predefined animations, like mobs.
- **Birds**: a little AI up close, predefined flights farther away.

This keeps the game stable. You can push *Mob Render Distance* and *Max Mobs* as far as you
like, but thousands of 3D models will lag. That is where **Simplified Entities** come in:
past a distance, mobs and players are drawn as flat 2D pictures of themselves instead of 3D
models, which takes far less rendering work. A mob 200 blocks away is only a few pixels
tall, so you hardly see the difference.

Pick a *Preset* (*Low*, *Normal*, *High*, *Ultra*) to set it all at once.

**Careful:** *Max Mobs* and *Mob Render Distance* both on *Unlimited* can crash the game.

## Multiplayer

Everything above works in single player. On a server, some of it needs the mod on the
server too:

| Feature | Single player | World opened to LAN | Server with the mod | Server without the mod |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mobs on the horizon | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Only the mobs you met yourself |
| Players at any distance | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| Players who logged off | ✓ | ✓ | ✓, even those who left before you joined | Only those you saw leave |
| Birds | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

- **Server with the mod**: put the same jar in the server's `mods/` folder (with the Fabric
  API on Fabric and Quilt). Nothing else to set up. It shares the players and the mobs with
  everyone who has the mod.
- **World opened to LAN**: nothing to do, the host's game is the server.
- Players without the mod can still join and play as usual.
- Every player's position is shared with the players who have the mod: tell your players.

## Getting started

1. Install the file of your game version and loader (see *Requirements*). Voxy and
   Distant Horizons are optional.
2. Keep the default settings: they are the best balance. *Restore Defaults* puts them back.

The settings are on the Options screen, in Mod Menu, in Sodium's *Video Settings*
(Sodium 0.8 and later) or with `/livinghorizon config`.

## Commands

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/livinghorizon` | Lists the players followed and says whether the server shares positions |
| `/livinghorizon config` | Opens the settings |
| `/livinghorizon scan` | Scans the mobs around you again, if ever needed (single player; done on its own the first time you open a world) |
| `/livinghorizon bird <kind>` | Calls a group of birds: `geese`, `gulls`, `ducks`, `raptors`... |

## Requirements

- Minecraft 1.20 to 1.21.11 and 26.1 to 26.3.
- Fabric or Quilt (with [Fabric API](https://modrinth.com/mod/fabric-api)), NeoForge or Forge.
- [Voxy](https://modrinth.com/mod/voxy) or [Distant Horizons](https://modrinth.com/mod/distanthorizons): optional. The mod also works without them, at any render distance.
- [Mod Menu](https://modrinth.com/mod/modmenu): optional, on Fabric and Quilt.

## Compatibility

| Mod | Notes |
|---|---|
| [Voxy](https://modrinth.com/mod/voxy) | Mobs and players are visible on its terrain. |
| [Distant Horizons](https://modrinth.com/mod/distanthorizons) | From 2.3 on. Mobs and players are visible on its terrain. |
| Iris and shader packs | Photon and Complementary checked. |
| [Sodium](https://modrinth.com/mod/sodium) | From 0.8, the settings are also in Sodium's *Video Settings*. |
| GeckoLib | Works. From 1.21.2 to 1.21.8 its mobs are not drawn far away yet ([#67](https://github.com/MC-Skin-Creator/living-horizon/issues/67)). |

Mobs from other mods should just work; mods still to check are tracked under the
[`mod compatibility`](https://github.com/MC-Skin-Creator/living-horizon/labels/mod%20compatibility)
label.
